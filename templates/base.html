📖 Mortgage Terms Explained Simply

LTV (Loan-to-Value)

What it is: How much you're borrowing compared to the property's value.

How it works: LTV = (Loan Amount ÷ Property Value) × 100

Example: £200,000 house, £40,000 deposit = £160,000 loan = 80% LTV

Why it matters: Lower LTV = Better interest rates! Banks prefer you to have a bigger deposit.

💡 Simple version: If LTV is 80%, you're borrowing 80p for every £1 the house is worth.

Interest Rate

What it is: The yearly cost of borrowing money, shown as a percentage.

Example: 4.5% rate on £150,000 = You pay £6,750 in interest per year

Why it matters: Even 0.5% difference = thousands of pounds over 25 years!

💡 Simple version: Think of it as the "rental fee" for borrowing the bank's money.

Monthly Payment

What it is: The amount you pay to the bank every month.

What's included: Part goes to paying back the loan, part goes to interest.

Why it matters: This is what comes out of your bank account each month!

💡 Simple version: Like rent, but you're buying the house bit by bit.

TRUE Cost

What it is: The REAL total amount you'll pay over the full mortgage term.

What's included: All interest + product fees + booking fees - cashback

Example: £150,000 loan might actually cost £220,000 over 25 years!

Why it matters: The cheapest rate isn't always the cheapest total cost!

💡 Simple version: This shows the FULL price tag, not just the advertised rate.

Product Fee (Arrangement Fee)

What it is: A one-time fee the bank charges to set up your mortgage.

Typical range: £0 - £2,000

When you pay: Usually added to the loan or paid upfront

Why it matters: A low rate with a £2,000 fee might cost MORE than a higher rate with no fee!

💡 Simple version: The bank's "setup charge" - like a booking fee.

Credit Score

What it is: A number (300-850) that shows how reliable you are at repaying debt.

Ranges:

  • 720-850 = Excellent (best rates)
  • 680-719 = Good
  • 620-679 = Fair
  • 580-619 = Poor
  • 300-579 = Very Poor (specialist lenders only)

Why it matters: Better score = Lower rates = Save thousands!

💡 Simple version: Your financial report card that banks check before lending.

Bad Credit

What it is: A history of missed payments, defaults, CCJs, or bankruptcy.

What counts as bad credit:

  • Missed payments (credit cards, loans, bills)
  • CCJs (County Court Judgements)
  • Defaults or IVAs
  • Bankruptcy or repossession

Good news: Specialist lenders WILL lend to you (we list 12 of them!)

💡 Simple version: Past money troubles - but you can still get a mortgage!

Specialist Lender

What it is: Banks that lend to people with bad credit or unusual situations.

Examples: Pepper Money, Bluestone, Kensington, Together Money

Rates: Usually 5.5%-7% (vs 4.5%-5% for mainstream)

Why it matters: They say YES when big banks say NO!

💡 Simple version: The helpful banks that give people a second chance.

Fixed Rate Mortgage

What it is: Your interest rate stays the same for a set period (usually 2-5 years).

Pros: Payments never change - easy to budget!

Cons: Might miss out if rates drop

💡 Simple version: Like a contract - your rate is locked in and won't go up.

Deposit

What it is: The chunk of money you pay upfront to buy the house.

Minimum: Usually 5% (but bigger = better rates)

Example: £200,000 house needs £10,000 minimum (5%)

Why it matters: Bigger deposit = Lower LTV = Cheaper mortgage!

💡 Simple version: Your skin in the game - shows banks you're serious.

Mortgage Term

What it is: How long you take to pay back the loan (in years).

Common terms: 25, 30, or 35 years

Shorter term: Higher monthly payments BUT less interest overall

Longer term: Lower monthly payments BUT more interest overall

💡 Simple version: Like choosing monthly vs yearly Netflix - longer = smaller payments but costs more in the end.

Cashback

What it is: Free money the bank gives you for taking their mortgage!

Typical amount: £0 - £500

The catch: Usually comes with slightly higher rates

Why it matters: Can help pay for moving costs or solicitor fees

💡 Simple version: A welcome bonus for choosing that lender.

Minimum Income

What it is: The least amount you need to earn per year to qualify.

Mainstream banks: Usually £20,000 - £30,000+

Specialist lenders: Some accept £10,000+

Why it matters: Banks want to make sure you can afford the payments!

💡 Simple version: Proof you earn enough to pay the mortgage each month.

Total Interest

What it is: ALL the interest you'll pay over the full mortgage term.

Example: Borrow £150,000, pay back £250,000 = £100,000 in interest!

Why it matters: This is where banks make their money!

💡 Simple version: The total "rental fee" for borrowing the bank's money.

Lender

What it is: The bank or building society giving you the mortgage.

Types:

  • Mainstream: HSBC, Barclays, Santander (strict, best rates)
  • Building Societies: Nationwide, Coventry (more flexible)
  • Specialist: Pepper Money, Bluestone (accept bad credit)
💡 Simple version: Who you're borrowing money from.
